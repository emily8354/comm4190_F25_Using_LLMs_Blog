Intro

PROMPT: I will be in Hong Kong for around two weeks in late December to early January. My family is from Hong Kong and I went a lot as a kid, but haven’t been in a while. Can you help me understand the areas of Hong Kong and what there is to do in each place? Can you also suggest some things to do there?

I’d be happy to help you rediscover Hong Kong! Let me break down the main areas and what makes each distinct:

Hong Kong Island

Central and Admiralty are the main business districts with luxury shopping, but also home to the Mid-Levels Escalator (world’s longest outdoor covered escalator system) and SoHo’s dining scene. Nearby, the Peak offers stunning views accessible by the historic Peak Tram.

Sheung Wan blends old and new - traditional dried seafood shops, herbal medicine stores, and antique dealers alongside trendy cafes and galleries. The Man Mo Temple here is worth visiting.

Wan Chai has evolved from its gritty past into a dining and nightlife hub, while Causeway Bay is Hong Kong’s busiest shopping district with everything from luxury malls to street markets.

The south side (Stanley, Repulse Bay, Shek O) offers beaches and a more relaxed vibe - Stanley Market is popular for souvenirs and casual dining by the waterfront.

Kowloon

Tsim Sha Tsui has the iconic Victoria Harbour waterfront, Avenue of Stars, museums (Space Museum, Art Museum, History Museum), and Nathan Road’s “Golden Mile” of shops.

Mong Kok is intense and vibrant - famous for street markets like the Ladies’ Market, sneaker street, and goldfish market. Yau Ma Tei nearby has the Temple Street Night Market.

New Territories

Sai Kung is known for seafood restaurants and access to hiking trails and beaches. The Ten Thousand Buddhas Monastery in Sha Tin is unique with its hillside statues.

Outlying Islands

Lantau has the Big Buddha and Po Lin Monastery, plus the fishing village of Tai O. Lamma and Cheung Chau offer slower-paced island life with good hiking and seafood.

Suggestions for your visit:

  • Take the Star Ferry between Kowloon and HK Island at sunset
  • Hike the Dragon’s Back trail (consistently rated one of Asia’s best urban hikes)
  • Explore the old neighborhoods like Sham Shui Po for authentic local life
  • Check out PMQ (former police quarters turned creative hub) in Central
  • Eat at a cha chaan teng for nostalgic Hong Kong cafe culture
  • Visit during New Year’s Eve for the spectacular harbour fireworks

PROMPT: How far are all of these places from each other? How do people get around?

Hong Kong is surprisingly compact and has one of the world’s best public transportation systems - everything is very accessible!

Distances and travel times:

Hong Kong Island to Kowloon is just across the harbour - the Star Ferry takes about 10 minutes, or the MTR (subway) is 2-5 minutes depending on which stations. Central to Tsim Sha Tsui is literally a 10-minute journey.

Within Hong Kong Island, Central to Causeway Bay is about 5-10 minutes by MTR. Central to Stanley (south side) takes 30-40 minutes by bus.

Within Kowloon, Tsim Sha Tsui to Mong Kok is about 10 minutes by MTR.

To the New Territories, you’re looking at 30-45 minutes from central areas. For example, Central to Sai Kung takes about 45 minutes (MTR + minibus).

Lantau Island is about 30-35 minutes from Central via the Airport Express or Tung Chung line, then a cable car or bus to the Big Buddha. Other outlying islands require ferries - 30-60 minutes depending on the island.

Getting around:

The MTR is the backbone - clean, efficient, air-conditioned, with English signage. Trains run every few minutes. Get an Octopus card (rechargeable smart card) - it works on MTR, buses, trams, ferries, minibuses, and even at convenience stores.

Buses go everywhere the MTR doesn’t, including scenic routes along the coast. Double-decker buses offer great views from the upper deck.

Trams (“ding dings”) run along Hong Kong Island’s north shore - they’re slow but iconic and cheap, great for sightseeing.

Minibuses (green or red) fill gaps but can be confusing for visitors since routes aren’t always clearly marked.

Taxis are relatively affordable - just note that Hong Kong Island, Kowloon, and New Territories have different colored cabs with different coverage areas.

Star Ferry is a must-do experience, not just transportation.

Google Maps works excellently in Hong Kong for public transport directions. You’ll find getting around is really straightforward - the city is designed for easy navigation without a car!

PROMPT: I will be staying in Mid Levels. How can I get places from there? Can you also suggest specific restaurants, shops, etc. for me to try and visit?

Great location! Mid-Levels is a residential area on the hillside above Central, so you’re very centrally positioned.

Getting around from Mid-Levels:

The Mid-Levels Escalator is your main artery - it runs downhill in the morning (6-10am) and uphill the rest of the day until midnight. It connects you to Central, SoHo, and Sheung Wan. You can hop on/off at different levels to explore the neighborhoods along the way.

To reach the MTR, you’ll typically walk or take the escalator down to Central, Sheung Wan, or Admiralty stations (10-20 minutes depending on where you are in Mid-Levels). From there, you have access to the entire MTR network.

Buses run through Mid-Levels - routes like the 3B, 12, 13, 23, 40 connect you to different parts of the island. Taxis are also easy to hail.

Specific recommendations:

Restaurants to try:

Dim sum: Lin Heung Tea House (Central) for old-school trolley-style dim sum, or Tim Ho Wan (multiple locations) - the famous Michelin-starred affordable dim sum. Luk Yu Tea House (Central) is historic and atmospheric.

Cha chaan teng (Hong Kong cafes): Kam Wah Cafe (Mong Kok) for pineapple buns, Tsui Wah (multiple locations) for classics like crispy bun with condensed milk, Australian Dairy Company (Jordan) for scrambled eggs and toast.

Roast goose: Yat Lok (Central) or Kam’s Roast Goose (Wan Chai) - both Michelin-recognized.

Wonton noodles: Mak’s Noodle (Central) or Tsim Chai Kee (Central/multiple locations).

Dai pai dong (open-air food stalls): Under the Bridge Spicy Crab in Wan Chai, or Sing Heung Yuen (Central) for their tomato beef noodles.

Seafood: Lamma Island has numerous waterfront restaurants where you pick your seafood from tanks - Rainbow Seafood or Lamma Hilton are popular. In Sai Kung, walk along the waterfront and choose from many options.

International/modern: The neighborhoods around your area (SoHo especially) are packed with options - Yardbird (yakitori),掛好 Kai Gai (modern Chinese), Little Bao (inventive buns), Chachawan (Thai).

Markets and shopping:

Temple Street Night Market (Yau Ma Tei) - classic night market experience with fortune tellers, street food, and trinkets.

Ladies Market (Mong Kok) - clothing, accessories, souvenirs.

Cat Street/Upper Lascar Row (Sheung Wan) - antiques and curios, right in your neighborhood.

PMQ (Central) - design and local crafts in a revitalized heritage building.

Sham Shui Po - fabric market on Ki Lung Street, button/ribbon shops, electronics at Golden Computer Arcade, very local neighborhood.

Stanley Market - touristy but scenic waterfront setting for souvenirs and casual browsing.

Specific experiences:

Pak Tai Temple in Wan Chai for a local temple experience, or Wong Tai Sin Temple in Kowloon (very popular, ornate).

Nan Lian Garden (Diamond Hill) - beautiful classical Chinese garden, free entry, next to the Chi Lin Nunnery.

Tai Kwun (Central) - beautifully restored former police station/prison turned arts and heritage center.

Goldfish Market (Mong Kou) - a street full of shops selling goldfish and aquarium supplies, very Hong Kong.

Since you’re there over New Year’s, definitely catch the harbour fireworks on New Year’s Eve - you can watch from Tsim Sha Tsui waterfront or from various spots on Hong Kong Island. Get there early as it gets very crowded!
